# Map editor

`FafEditor_x64.exe` (x64) / `FafEditor.exe` (x86) is the map editor: its own program, built from the same engine code as the game. It draws through Direct3D 12 or Vulkan only, and plays maps inside its own window.

*Snapshot: 10/11/2026. Opening maps (from the game's folders, any folder, or the FAF vault), the 3D edit view, the terrain, texture, water, prop, decal, marker and unit tools, saving and packaging, Check, and Play inside the editor.*

## Building and running

```powershell
.\build.ps1 -Editor        # builds only the editor's target, copies FafEditor_x64.exe (+ .pdb) to bin_x64
.\build.ps1 -Editor -Configuration Debug
cd C:\ProgramData\FAForever\bin_x64
.\FafEditor_x64.exe /init init_faf.lua /log C:\ProgramData\FAForever\logs\FafEditor_x64.log
```

In VS Code, the Run view has "Editor (x64, Debug)" and "Editor (x64, Release)": F5 builds the editor's target of `clang-x64-debug` or `clang-x64-release`, copies it to `bin_x64` and starts it under the debugger with `/init init_local_development.lua` (the Lua of the FA checkout, as the engine's own F5 entries use). "Editor (x64, no build)" starts what is already there. Only running editors are stopped before the copy; the game is left alone. The `.vscode` files are local (not in git).

`-Editor` never replaces the game's `main_x64.exe`, its headless or the DXC DLLs in the run folder (the editor carries its own DXC); a Profile build is copied as `FafEditor_x64_profile.exe`, and `-Out` also copies it to `out\<arch>\`. The editor reads the game data the way the game does: `/init <file>` names the init file (FAF's `init_faf.lua`), else `SupComDataPath.lua` next to the exe.

## Play

The toolbar over the panels starts with **Play** (F5), then New, Open, Save, Save as, Undo and Redo, Grid, Snap and the grid's step (Grid draws the cell edges on the ground around the cursor; Snap puts markers and props placed or dragged in a cell's middle, while mass and hydrocarbon points always sit on half units as the game's saves place them; `/grid`, `/snap`, `/gridstep <n>` for test runs), and names the open map at its right ("edited" while it has unsaved changes), then the **UI scale**: one joined group, - / the scale in percent / +, stepping 75 to 200% (the middle resets to 100%). The panels' sizes are scaled from the style made for the screen's DPI (`ImGuiStyle::ScaleAllSizes`) and the text by `style.FontScaleMain`; sizes the panels lay out by hand go through `Em()` (`ui/EdUiScale.h`, pixels at the 16 px base font). The scale is kept in `layout.ini` as `[FafEditor][Ui] Scale=<x>`, the same for every map; `/uiscale <x>` sets it for a test run. The panels' tab titles get 9 x 5 px of padding (ImGui's default is 4 x 3). Pick a map in the Map panel (each map by its title, then its folder name; type to filter; double-click opens it for editing) or in Play > Map, and press **Play** or F5. The map loads and plays inside the editor's view, between the panels, with the game's own interface. **Stop** or F5 returns to the editor. So does the game's own way out of a session: its menu's exit, quitting from the score screen, and "Exit to Windows", which stops the play instead of closing the editor. View > Panels while playing (on by default) hides the panels during play when turned off, so the game takes the whole window. The window's title says what plays. **Editor view / Game view** (F6, or the switch beside Stop, while a play runs): the editor's view of its map comes in front of the play, with its camera, tools and overlays, while the game runs on behind it; F6 again gives the game's view back as it was. The renderer draws one map at a time (`sWldMap`): the editor's while its view is in front, the session's again after; an engine addition, `moho::gViewportOnlyView`, has the viewport draw only the editor's world view while the session's stay registered, `ren_Ui` leaves the game's interface undrawn, and mouse and keys the editor's view doesn't take are kept from the game (a click must not order its units). The session keeps its own map for everything it does (`CWldSession::mWldMap`), so nothing of the play changes. The game's own units show in the editor's view, and so do the map's props and placed units (WPE.51): between plays the view draws them with the rules the editor keeps (`RULE_PeekKept`), and in front of a play with the same rules lent to it (`RULE_PeekLent`, an engine addition), which become the kept ones again when it ends, so what the view drew stays good past the play's end; before the next play takes the rules the view lets go of them, as between plays. The map's placed units are the map's (what its `_save.lua` places): the play's copies of them are game units, shown as well, at the same places until they move. On SCMP_009 two plays in a row, each ended behind the editor's view (`/playseconds 12 /playcycles 2 /playeditorview 4,0`), drew 5182 props and 43 units in front of each, with nothing missing and no error. A play that ends behind the editor's view leaves the editor on its map. Each view keeps its camera across the switch: the game's is untouched, and the editor's comes back where it was left for the same map (a newly opened map starts framed whole). `/playeditorview <on>,<off>[,<on again>]` (seconds into a `/play`) for test runs; each switch logs both cameras. On SCMP_009 the game's camera was at 672.5,346.5 zoom 40 before and after, and the editor's, zoomed and dragged to 159.0,331.9 at 561.3 before the switch, came back there. **The game's units in the editor's view** (`view/EdLiveUnits.cpp`, `ui/EdLiveUnitPanel.cpp`): with the Select tool a click picks the play's own unit nearest it (Ctrl adds or takes out), a red ring marks each, and a drag warps them by as much (the sim's `Warp`, on the ground there). The Properties panel then shows the game unit: its icon, name, blueprint, army and entity id; its health (a slider, Full) and veterancy (FAF's `SetVeterancy`); its blueprint's toggles as the order panel has them (shield, intel, jamming, production, stealth and cloak "on" while their script bit is clear); its enhancements by slot (ticked to add, `CreateEnhancement`, at once; unticked through the `<name>Remove` enhancement where the blueprint has one); Kill and Remove. The state is read through the game's UI Lua (`GetUnitById`, its blueprint, `GetScriptBit`, `EnhancementCommon.GetEnhancements`); each change is the game's own call in its sim (`SimLua`; the editor's plays have cheats on), and none touches the map's files. "Game units" beside the switch hides them in the editor's view (not drawn, not picked). `/playlivetest` (with `/playeditorview`) for test runs: SCMP_009's Cybran commander, 10000/10000 at 672.5,346.5 with none of its 10 enhancements, became 5023/10000 at 692.5,346.5 with Teleporter. **Editing while it plays** (investigated for WPE.39; the editor's own solo playback, never multiplayer): a play is the game's own session, loaded from the map's files into a world map of its own (`EdPlay::StartSession`, `EditorStartPlay`), and its sim holds a copy of the heights (`STIMap::STIMap(STIMap*)`, 0x00577890, copies the samples). The sim is the heights' source: each beat hands its height field to the user side (`SSyncData::mTerrainUpdate`), and `CWldTerrainRes::SyncTerrain` copies the changed rectangles into the rendered terrain, but only those the camera sees, zoomed in past `ren_SyncTerrainLOD`, and visible to the focus army. `/playterrainpoke x,z,size,height` (with `/play`) proves the sim half: on Seton's Clutch `SimLua FlattenMapRect(502, 502, 20, 20, 120)` left the sim's height at the centre 120 (its own log) while the rendered height stayed 18.48 (the centre was out of the camera). So live edits can go in: heights through the sim, between beats (as `FlattenMapRect`: the samples, the changed rectangles, units on the area re-seated), with the rendered terrain synced for the edited rectangles whatever the camera; texture layers, masks, decals, water and lighting straight into the session's world map (they are only the renderer's); the session's units shown or hidden by culling their meshes. The path data isn't rebuilt by a height change (`FlattenMapRect` doesn't either). **Heights made in the editor's view reach the play** (WPE.40, `view/EdLiveTerrain.cpp`): each rectangle a height tool, undo or redo changes is copied into the session's rendered terrain at once (`SetElevationRectRaw`, then `NotifyMapChanges` one sample wider for the normals), whatever its camera sees, and queued for the sim, which takes it at the start of its next beat on its own thread through an engine addition, `moho::gSimBeforeBeat` (`moho/sim/SimEditorHook.h`, called first in `Sim::AdvanceBeat` and empty in every exe but the editor's). The queue and the hook go when the play stops. A play of another size than the map's is left alone. `/playheighttest` (with `/playeditorview`) for test runs: a raise 25 units beside SCMP_009's first commander took the editor's height at 698,346 from 18.82 to 48.82, the play's drawn terrain read 48.82 there at once, its sim 18.82 -> 48.82 at the next beat (its own log), and the sim's Lua `GetTerrainHeight` 48.82 (that run's version of the test). After the heights, the sim's path clusters over the changed area (4 cells wider) are marked dirty (`PathTables::DirtyClusters`, so they are worked out again from the new slopes), and the land and seabed units standing on it are put back on the ground as `Sim::FlattenMapRect` (0x0074B120) does: a moving unit through its motion's next surface pass, a structure by a warp in place (WPE.47). The test now raises under SCMP_009's first commander: it stood at 48.68 on ground raised from 18.68 to 48.68; with `/playnoreseat` (the re-seat off) it stayed at 18.68, under the ground. No test drives a path across the changed ground yet. **Lighting, water, masks and layers reach the play too** (WPE.41): while the editor's view is in front of a play, the document holds the play's map (`EdDocument::SetLiveMap`), and each lighting and water edit (and its undo) is written into it as into the editor's (sun, ambience, shadow fill, specular, bloom, fog; the water's levels, shader numbers and textures, and its water map). The water's levels also go into the sim at its next beat through the same hook as the heights, so what floats and what is under water follows them. Mask strokes send the same rows to the play's terrain (`UpdateStratumMask`) when its masks are the map's size, and a layer's texture change goes into the play's strata. Decal edits go in too (WPE.49): each placement, path, deletion and move, and their undo, is the change between two snapshots of the decals, and `MirrorDecals` (`tools/EdDecals.cpp`) makes that change in the play's decal manager, matching decals by their saved fields rather than by index (`DecalKey`), so a decal the play loaded from the map's file is found by what it is. `/playdecaltest` (with `/playeditorview`) for test runs: on SCMP_009 (2236 decals in both) a decal added beside the commander was in the play at once (2237); the map's own boulder decal moved 40 units was gone from its old place in the play and there at the new one; putting the map's decals back as undo does left the play at 2236 with neither. `/playlooktest` (with `/playeditorview`) for test runs: on SCMP_009 the play's sun colour went 1.38,1.29,1.14 -> 2.00,0.40,0.30, its drawn water 17.50 -> 23.50, its sim's water 17.50 -> 23.50 (the sim's `GetSurfaceHeight` 23.5 over ground at 6.09 at 150,150), and its stratum 4 mask at the painted texel 140 -> 255.

The Map panel sets the play up, the way FAF's `/map` quick start does (`SinglePlayerLaunch.lua`) but with these choices:

- **Solo** (default) or **Against AI** with one of the game's AI types for every other army. Solo is the faster start: with no AI there is no AI map analysis (SCMP_009, second play: 2.3 s to the game solo, 4.5 s against "rush").
- **Faction**: random or one of the game's factions.
- **Fog**: fog of war ("explored") or none. The sim fixes fog when the game starts, so it applies from the next Play.
- **Keep this setup for this map**: on, the map keeps its own setup, saved at each Play; off, it plays with the setup of the last Play on any map. Picking a map takes its own setup or the last one. Kept in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\FAForever\Editor\play_setups.ini` by map folder name without its `.vNNNN` version, AI and faction by their keys. Test runs from the command line read it but don't write it. **Quick start** (on by default, kept with the setup): the commanders are there and take orders at once instead of FAF's warp-in, which hides each one for 3 s (`ScenarioUtilities.CommanderWarpDelay`) and then plays 2.1 s of effect (`CommandUnit.PlayCommanderWarpInEffect`), and the camera starts over yours instead of FAF's zoom to it a second in. As the play starts the editor sends the sim three short `SimLua` lines (its plays have cheats on): each commander shown as the effect's end leaves it (its blueprint's `WarpInEffect.HideBones` hidden again), its order queue opened, and a `PlayCommanderWarpInEffect` of its own that does nothing, since FAF looks that call up on the unit when the 3 s are over. The camera is framed on your commander in C++ as `UIZoomTo` (0x00867240) frames it; the game's `GetArmyAvatars` called from the editor's Lua fails LuaPlus's same-state check when it fills its table. With Prebuilt units on FAF has no warp-in, and only the camera is moved. On SCMP_009 the commander was shown at tick 7 and the camera was over it at the first playing frame; `/playnoquickstart` gives FAF's own start for comparison.
- **Slots** (the Play button's arrow, or the Map panel): each army slot as You, an AI (any AI type, AI mods' included: FAF's `lua/AI/CustomAIs_v2`) or empty, with its team (armies of one team start allied, as the lobby's teams) and an AI's faction; no You row watches the AIs as an observer. Victory, unit cap and the AI cheat multiplier as the lobby sets them, and sim mods to play with. **Profiles** keep a setup by name; the built-in ones are Solo sandbox, You vs 1 AI, 2 v 2 with AI, Free for all with AI and Watch 4 AI. SCMP_009 with M28AI plays without errors; 2 v 2 starts armies 1 and 3, and 2 and 4, allied. `/playprofile <n>`, `/playlogarmies` for test runs.
- **Hide the fog** and **Observer view**, during play: the first leaves out the fog's shading while you keep your army (a render-only switch, `ren_FogOfWarHidden`, as `ren_FogOfWar` is set by the sim every beat); the second switches to an observer's view (every army, no fog) and back. Editor plays have cheats on for this, without changing the `debug.enable_debug_facilities` preference the game reads. `/playnofog` for test runs.

Campaign maps go through FAF's own campaign setup. No replay is written.

How fast a play starts:

- The game's rules (every unit, projectile and effect blueprint, about 1.5 s to build for FAF) are built on a thread of their own when the editor starts, for the mods a skirmish uses (`RULE_PrepareKept`), and each play's rules are kept for the next one (`RULE_SetKeepBetweenSessions`). A Play pressed before they are ready waits for them. The game builds them for every session.
- Loading shows one line of text in the view ("Loading Seton's Clutch ...") instead of the game's faction movie, "IN TRANSIT" screen and loading tips, and the game's panels come in as soon as the map is ready instead of after the "IN TRANSIT" picture fades (`InstallLoadingView`, through `moho::gUIBeforeStartGameUI`).
- Solo, a Play 5 s after the editor starts reaches the game in about 2.3 s on SCMP_009 and 2.7 s on Alpha 7 Quarantine (SCMP_021, 40 km); later plays in 1.0-1.3 s and 1.6-2.0 s (Direct3D 12, Vulkan). On SCMP_021 two engine changes do it: the 3584 building skirts its start flattens update the terrain normal map one tile at a time for all of them (`IWldTerrainRes::NotifyMapChanges`, 30 ms where one update per skirt took 1.6 to 3.3 s), and a whole-map normal-map rebuild after the map changed is encoded on 4 threads as the loader's (0.4 s, from 1.6 s).

`/play` takes several maps separated by commas; the runs take them in turn.

The window is always one maximized, movable, resizable window with a menu bar (File, View, Play), whatever the game's display options say; the game's own display option doesn't change it either. The panel layout is kept in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\FAForever\Editor\layout.ini`; View > Reset layout restores the default.

**Opening a map** (File > Open map, Ctrl+O, the toolbar's Open or the Map panel's "Open a map...", all the Maps window below; File > Open Recent for the maps opened last, newest first, numbered as VS Code numbers them, with "Clear recently opened") opens it into the editor's document on a thread of its own: the `.scmap` as the game loads it, `_scenario.lua` and `_save.lua`, each in the editor's own Lua state, apart from any play. The Info panel (View > Info; View > Reset layout docks it beside the Map panel) shows what was read: size, height field, water, terrain shader, the ten texture layers, props, decals, markers by type, armies and the map's files. Between plays the game's view shows the opened map (terrain, texture layers, decals, water) through the game's own renderer, as the original editor did: the editor puts its map in the renderer's map (`REN_SetWldMap`) with a world view and a camera of its own (`view/EdMapScene.cpp`), and takes them away before a play's session takes over. Camera: middle mouse button drag orbits, Shift + middle drag or right drag pans (the ground follows the cursor), WASD or the arrow keys move (Shift faster), the wheel zooms toward the point under the cursor without changing the camera's tilt, Home frames the whole map. The map's props are drawn as mesh instances from the blueprints of the rules the editor keeps for its next play (`RULE_PeekKept`), once those are built after startup.

**Terrain brush:** with the Terrain tool, the left mouse button held over the opened map paints heights with the panel's mode (Raise, Lower, Smooth, Flatten to the height under the stroke's first point, Set height to the panel's height, Noise, Terrace to the nearest step, Plane through three points Ctrl+clicked on the terrain, Stamp: a height image added over the brush's square once per click, white adding the stamp height times strength, Erode: thermal erosion, where the ground between two neighbours is steeper than the panel's talus slope height moves from the higher to the lower until it isn't, each pair's exchange equal and opposite so ground is moved downhill and none is made, at up to 60 full steps a second times strength, at most one a frame, since a change scaled like the other brushes' would round to nothing on gentle ground at 60 fps, Water erode: hydraulic erosion, droplets dropped at random in the brush (600 a second times strength) run downhill with a little inertia, take ground where they speed up and leave it where they slow or climb, and leave what they still carry where they stop, so channels and fans form and no ground is made; it runs around the cursor's copy of the brush and writes each changed sample to its whole symmetry group, rounding at random but evenly from a seed the stroke advances every step), radius, strength, falloff and symmetry (`tools/EdHeightBrush.cpp`). Limits leave alone the vertices outside a height range or steeper than a slope (judged at each symmetry group's first vertex, so copies stay identical). `/brush <mode>` (9 is Erode, 10 Water erode), `/erodetalus <slope>` and `/stamp <file>` set the mode, the talus and the stamp image for test runs; each stroke logs how many samples it changed. On SCMP_009 a three-second Erode stroke on the rocky ground at the view's lower left changed 442 samples at the default talus 0.6 and 824 at 0.2, the hollow under the cursor filling from 26.05 to 27.09. A four-second Water erode stroke there changed 1326 samples. Symmetry is exact: each group of mirrored samples gets one new height, computed from its first member, written to all of them, so mirrored halves stay identical and a stroke on a mirror line applies once (GoogleTest `faf_editor_brush_tests`). N-way turns (2 to 16 copies, the panel offers 3 to 8) can't be exact, as their copies fall between vertices: the first sector (from the map's centre towards +x, turning towards +z) is painted, and every other vertex the brush covers, plus 2 vertices past its edge, takes the height at its point turned back into the first sector, read between vertices; 4 turns are exact. The diagonal and turn modes paint without symmetry on a map that isn't square. The symmetry's mirror lines are drawn along the ground with a translucent wall standing on each (the turn centre as a vertical axis, the sectors' edges for N-way turns), and the brush shows as rings on the terrain at every copy (white at the cursor, orange at the mirrored ones; the inner ring is where the falloff starts), drawn through the renderer's editor hook (`ED_SetHook`). Edit > Undo and Redo, Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y (or Ctrl+Shift+Z) in the view or the panels, take back whole strokes, byte for byte (`undo/EdHeightHistory.cpp`: the 32 x 32 tiles a stroke touched, before and after; 256 MB at most, the oldest strokes go first).

**Texture layers** (Tools panel, Texture, Layers; `tools/EdTextureLibrary.cpp`): the map's ten slots (lower, strata 1 to 8, upper), each with a thumbnail of its albedo texture, its name and its size in game units (a drag sets it; the normal map keeps its ratio to the albedo). Clicking a thumbnail opens the texture browser: every `.dds` under `/env/<biome>/layers/` the game data has, grouped by biome folder, searchable, as thumbnails (64 x 64, decoded a few a frame through the engine's image codec); picking one sets the slot's albedo and, when a `_normals.dds` lies beside it, its normal map; Empty clears a stratum. The terrain loads the new texture at once (`StratumMaterial::SetSizeTo`) and Save writes it. Slot changes undo and redo with the strokes (a size drag is one step). `/layer <slot>,<albedo path>[,<size>]` and `/browse <slot>` do the same for test runs.

**Texture painting** (Tools panel, Texture; `tools/EdMaskBrush.cpp`): the paint list shows the map's strata 1 to 8 by texture name (the lower layer lies under all; the upper one is blended by its own alpha). Paint raises the chosen stratum's weight, Erase lowers it, Smooth evens it out; "Lower the layers above" makes Paint lower the strata blended over the chosen one by as much, so it shows. The brush's radius, strength, falloff, symmetry and limits are the terrain brush's; symmetry is exact on the masks' texels as on heights (N-way turns paint the first sector and copy the nearest turned-back texel). Weights move by whole steps with a per-step dither, so weak strokes still reach full. Rules set a stratum to full where the ground lies in a height range and/or slope range, over the whole map, in order (Apply rules to whole map, one undo step). When the Texture tool first acts on a map, the terrain enters edit mode (`EnterEditMode`, as the original editor did: the masks become writable textures) and the masks are read into the editor (`GetStratumMask`); a stroke writes back only what it changed (`UpdateStratumMask`). Strokes undo and redo with the height strokes in one order. The paint list shows each stratum's share of the map, and "Show the layer's weight" colours the paint layer green over the ground where it lies (blocks of 4 x 4 mask texels, rebuilt after each change; `/layerweight` with `/masklayer` for test runs); under it, Clear empties the chosen stratum's mask, Import reads it from a grey image (PNG, BMP, TIFF, RAW or R16, resampled to the mask's size) and Export writes it (16-bit grey PNG, RAW or R16, the weight times 257), each one undo step. With the Texture tool the symmetry check compares the masks' texels (all eight layers at once) and the copy of the source part copies texels. The shader picker under the slots lists every technique of the game's `terrain` effect that it marks composite (33 with FAF's data: `TTerrain`, `TTerrainXP`, `Terrain000` ... `Terrain301`), the shaders a map may name; a change is one undo step, and the engine checks the name again before it draws (`tools/EdShaderSlots.cpp`: under each slot the panel says what the chosen shader makes of it, from FAF's `terrain.fx` layouts: the 0XX, 1XX and 2XX shaders keep stratum 8 for map-wide data, water depth, ambient occlusion and shadow, with the map's normals in its normal slot, and the upper slot for the layers' height and roughness, a macrotexture for Terrain000/050 and TTerrainXPExt, a specular map for Terrain001/051; Terrain301 and TerrainPBR keep stratum 8 a stratum and put the map's normals, water depth and shadow in the upper slot. The shader reads a slot as map-wide only when its size is above the map's, so its tile value is under 1, and the panel says whether that is on; the map-wide textures themselves are baked by FAF's map tools, not here). `/shader <name>` changes it for test runs. `/masklayer <1-8>` and `/maskrule <layer>,<low>,<high>` set the layer and apply a height rule for test runs; `/roundtrip` now also compares the eight masks texel for texel.

**Symmetry on open** (`DetectSymmetry`): maps don't store the symmetry they were made with, so while a map loads its heights are compared with their copies under each mode on a 128 x 128 grid of points (within half a game unit); of the modes where at least 97% match, the one with the most copies is set in the Tools panel, which names it with its match share. A flat map fits every mode and gets none. Over the 63 retail and test maps 24 get a mode (Seton's Clutch a half turn, Drake's Ravine both axes).

**Symmetry check** (Tools panel, Terrain, under Symmetry; `tools/EdSymmetryCheck.cpp`): Check compares every vertex with its copies under the chosen symmetry and marks the differing ones as red blocks on the terrain (Show differences), with a count and the largest difference; it is kept up to date after strokes, undo and redo. N-way turns compare a vertex with the height at its point turned back into the first sector and count differences above a quarter unit. "Copy the source part to the other copies" makes the heights symmetric from the part nearest the map's top-left corner (the first sector for turns), as one undo step. With the Texture tool it compares the masks (above); the props are checked by View > Check (below). `/symcheck <mode>[x<turns>][:repair]` with `/open <map>` runs the check (mode: the panel's symmetry, 0 None to 9 N-way) and the copy on the drawn map (GoogleTest `faf_editor_brush_tests`: a planted one-vertex difference is found and repaired in every mode).

**Height field import and export** (Tools panel, Terrain, Height field; `tools/EdHeightFile.cpp`): Import reads `.png`, `.bmp` or `.tif` through the Windows imaging component as 16-bit grey (8-bit images spread over 16 bits), or `.raw` / `.r16` (World Machine) as 16-bit little-endian words, square or of the map's size; it is resampled to the map's size and is one undo step. **Props** (Tools panel, Props): a searchable list of every prop blueprint the game's rules have (`GetPropBlueprints`), the chosen one's reclaim mass and energy, scale and LODs under the list, and in Single mode the prop itself under the cursor with its symmetric copies (drawn by the mesh renderer, at the turn the click will use: a random turn is drawn before the click), Single (one at a click), Scatter (props a second at random points inside the brush while the button is held) and Erase (those inside the brush; Shift+click erases in any mode), random turn, the brush's radius and symmetry: each prop gets its symmetric copies, each copy turned as the symmetry carries the cursor's heading. **Select tool**: a click picks the nearest prop (Ctrl adds), a drag moves the selection over the ground, Q and E turn it by 15 degrees, Delete or Edit > Delete selection removes it; selected props are ringed in yellow. **What becomes of a unit** (Properties, Unit; `doc/EdUnitFate.cpp`, `ui/EdUnitFatePanel.cpp`): a map's units are only data until something creates their group (FAF's `lua/sim/ScenarioUtilities.lua`): as a game starts, each army's `INITIAL` group becomes units (a civilian army's unless the lobby's Civilians option is "removed") and its `WRECKAGE` group becomes wrecks (`CreateWreckageUnit`: the unit made, its wreck put in its place, the unit removed at once); any other group is in a game only when the map's script creates it (`CreateArmyGroup(army, group, wreckage)` and its kin, read from the script: a name written `'Optional_Wreckage_' .. w` matches the groups it starts; a group made as wrecks for one lobby value and as units for another, as DualGap's civilian base, is "either"). DualGap's Salem destroyer in `Optional_Wreckage_2`: 1620 mass. For a script's group the **Lobby** row says which lobby option decides it and what each of its values does (`doc/EdGroupOptions.cpp`): the script is read as Lua (comments and long strings skipped), with the `if` / `elseif` / `else`, `for` and `while` blocks around each `CreateArmyGroup` and the calls of the function it is in (a few levels up), and the options it reads (`local x = ScenarioInfo.Options.key or 1`, or `ScenarioInfo.Options.key` itself); every combination of the deciding options' values from the map's `_options.lua` (an option there is `default` as an index into its values, as FAF's lobby reads it) is evaluated, and conditions on anything else (who plays a slot, factions) are "maybe". A name `'Optional_Wreckage_' .. w` in `for w = 2, optional_wreckage` is made on the turn w equals its number. An option the script reads but `_options.lua` doesn't offer is always the script's default, so its groups never appear: DualGap Adaptive v14 has its naval, middle, faction and civilian options commented out, and the panel shows those groups as never in a game, while `Optional_Wreckage_2` reads "Lobby option "Wreckage" (optional_wreckage): "disabled" (default): not created; "T3 wreckage": created as wrecks". In the Properties panel this is one row, **Appears**: a sentence that is also the choice ("When Wreckage = "T3 wreckage" (wreck)", "From the start, as a live unit", "Never: the lobby has no option optional_naval_wreckage" in amber), its tooltip the details above, and "Option..." beside it opening the Adaptive Map window on that option. The choice (`SpawnTargets`) edits it: from the start as a live unit (`INITIAL`) or a wreck (`WRECKAGE`), or any group the script creates for the unit's army, each with the first lobby setting that brings it in (`Wreckage = "T3 wreckage"`) and whether as wrecks or units; a pick moves the selected units of that army into the group (one undo step). A group no setting makes yet is offered with the `_options.lua` change that does, kept only when reading the script again with it shows the group created: the next level of a looped option adds a value after the option's own (their texts and help kept), and an option the script reads but the lobby lacks is written (disabled, then levels); that change is a second undo step. DualGap's destroyer moved to `Optional_Wreckage_3` added "level 2" to Wreckage, and its Lobby row then read "level 2": created as wrecks. The Adaptive panel's script groups show the same lines on hover. `/unitspawn <group>` with `/selectunit` for test runs. Its Wreck box shows the real state: for a unit of the start groups it moves the unit between `INITIAL` and `WRECKAGE` (one undo step); for a script's group it is shown, not changed (the script decides), with "Move to the start groups". A unit that becomes a wreck is drawn with the `<mesh>_wreck` blueprint FAF's Blueprints.lua makes for every unit (the Wreckage shader), as the game's wreck props are. **Saving** (`doc/EdMapFile.cpp`, `doc/EdDocument.cpp`):

- **File > Save** (Ctrl+S) writes the opened map's `.scmap` over its file, only when that file is in the vault (`<vault>/maps/...`): maps inside the game's archives or folders have no file of their own to save over and are saved as a new map folder instead. - **File > Save as new map folder...** asks for a folder name (the next version, `seton.v0002` after `seton.v0001`, is offered) and writes `<vault>/maps/<folder>`: every file of the map's folder, those named after the map renamed to the folder's name without its version, the `.lua` files' paths into the old folder pointed into the new one, and the edited `.scmap`. - The vault is the FAF client's (`custom_vault_path` in `fa_path.lua`, which the init file reads and mounts maps from) when this Windows user can write it; else the user's `Documents/My Games/Gas Powered Games/Supreme Commander Forged Alliance`, whose map folders the editor mounts itself at startup when the init file doesn't (`DISK_MountDirectoriesNow`, one rebuild of the file system), so downloads are there after a restart; or `/vault <folder>` on the command line.
- What is written: the file header, the preview image as read (byte for byte), the terrain as the game's own writer saves it (version 60: heights, the ten texture layers, water, decals, masks and the rest), then the props. A prop the editor didn't move keeps its record byte for byte; a moved one is written from its position and rotation with scale 1, which the game ignores. The masks are written with the classic DDS header (`ImageCodec::PreferOwnDds`): the game's Direct3D 9 decoder doesn't read the newer DX10 header. The normal map and the water maps are rebuilt from the terrain, so their bytes differ from the original file's.
- The terrain's images (the normal map, the two stratum masks, the water map) go back as the file had them where what they are made from is as it was at load (`doc/EdImageChunks.cpp`): the normal map when the heights are, the water map when the heights and the sea's levels are, the masks when their texels are; the engine would encode them again into other bytes (its DXT5, and a DDSD_LINEARSIZE header flag the originals don't set). So SCMP_009 saved unedited is byte for byte its original (9636487 bytes, 0 differ, where 35237 differed before), and after a height stroke only the normal and water maps are new.
- `/roundtrip <folder>` (with `/openall` or alone: every map; with `/open <map>`: the drawn map) saves each map under `<folder>`, reads it back, compares heights, water, shader, layers, props and decals, saves again and compares bytes, then closes. **The particle view** (`view/EdParticleViewport.cpp`): the Particle Editor's and the Browser's preview is a 3D view of its own, not the map: the effect alone over a plain ground with a grid (a line every game unit, stronger every five; the emitter's spot in orange), against a night, grey, sky or black background; drag to turn round it, the wheel to come closer, Front / Side / Top / Reset, Turn (the camera circles), and the emitter's height above the ground. The effect is emitted far off any map (-6000, -6000), and the view renders it with its own camera into a target of its own through the world particle renderer, at the start of the game head's frame (an engine addition, `moho::gViewportBeforeFrame`, null in the game), so the map's view never shows it and this view shows nothing of the map, its markers or helpers.

**Beams and trails** open from the same browser (and File > New beam / New trail): they have no curves, so the window shows their keys with what each does, the engine's default greyed for a key the file leaves out (RBeamBlueprint, RTrailBlueprint; writing a value adds the key, so a file keeps only what it sets). The game's own are written by hand, with `#` comments; they read and write back with the same data (all 103 beams and 184 trails of retail's effects.scd), keys in the file's order, comments dropped. The preview draws a beam from the view's centre, rising (at most 60 units long), for its Lifetime and again; a trail behind a point circling the centre 15 units out, a turn every 4 s, a segment a tick as `CEfxTrailEmitter::Tick` builds them. `/particleopen <game path>`, `/particlenew beam|trail`, `/particlecollapse` and `/particleframe <distance>` for test runs; the seraphim experimental laser hit, the quantum generator's beam and a new trail on SCMP_009 draw at the view's centre.

**Particle Browser** (View > Particle Browser, or File > Browse the game's effects in the Particle Editor; `ui/EdParticleBrowser.cpp`): every `.bp` under `/effects` (3,045 with FAF's data) as a grid of tiles, each the effect's particle texture over a dark ground with its colour ramp under it and a corner mark for its type (orange emitter, blue beam, green trail); files are read only as their tiles show, a few a frame. Filters: faction (by the name's prefix: aeon_, cybran_, uef_, seraphim_/ser_), kind (by words in the name: muzzle, hit, explosion, smoke, fire, trail, beam, water, shield, build, ambient, teleport), type, search. A click plays the effect in the browser's own preview (the magnified middle of the map's view, as the Particle Editor's) and at the middle of the map's view; Edit in the Particle Editor opens it there and closes the browser. Opening another effect in place of one with unsaved edits (a tile, File > New or Open) first asks: Save, Don't save or Cancel (`/particleswitch <game path>` for test runs). `/particlebrowser <search>` for test runs.

**Effect decorations** (`PlaceEffect`, `UpdateEffects` in `view/EdMapScene.cpp`; `WithScriptEffects` in `doc/EdSaveFile.cpp`): Place on the map in the Particle Browser or Editor puts an `Effect` marker at the middle of the view, with `effect` (the effect's path), `scale` and `height` (above the ground); the Markers tool moves it with the gizmo and the Properties panel edits it, and the editor plays each one live where it stands. The game has no effect at a point (only CreateEmitterAtEntity, OnEntity, AtBone), so when the map is saved with any Effect markers the editor writes a `MapEditorEffects` function into its `_script.lua`, called from OnPopulate after `ScenarioUtils.InitializeArmies()`: for each Effect marker an entity of its own (`Entity({})`, army 255), warped to the marker, the effect created on it (`CreateEmitterAtEntity(entity, -1, effect)`, scaled when asked), and one log line; with none, the block and its call are taken out again. Seton's Clutch with the seraphim experimental laser hit placed, saved as a vault copy and test-run: the game logged `map editor: 1 effect decorations`. An effect of the map's own folder plays only in games started from FAF's lobby (its blueprint loader reads the map's folder). `/placeeffect <game path>` for test runs.

**Vault search filters** (`ui/EdMapBrowser.cpp` DrawVaultFilters, `doc/EdFafApi.cpp` VaultQueryFilter): the FAF client's map vault filters (its MapVaultController): the name (the search box), author, uploaded from/to (the latest version's update time, YYYY-MM-DD), width and height (any of 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 40, 80 km), max players from/to (0-16), average review score from/to (0-5), only ranked; sort by any of the client's sortable map properties (times played, name, author, uploaded, updated, max players, width, height, folder, ranked, description, version id), ascending or descending; Reset, Save query and Favorites (saved queries, in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\FAForever\Editorault_queries.txt`), Display search query (the filter as sent). Each condition is written as the client's QBuilder writes it (`=="*text*"`, `=in=(...)`, `=ge=`, `=le=`, `=gt=`/`=lt=` dates). "Dual", 10 km, 8 players, ranked: 4 maps from FAF's API. The filters show before signing in as well.

A window pulled out of the dock layout (the Material Editor, the Maps window, any panel) takes the mouse over the 3D view where it covers it; before, a click on such a window over the view went to the view (a height stroke under a floating window).

The Tools panel is laid out as the plan's mockup: a strip of tool icons and the chosen tool's settings (brush modes, size, strength, falloff, the symmetry modes with their diagrams). The settings are kept but act on a map only from the tool stages of the plan.

## How it is built

- **Target:** `src/editor/CMakeLists.txt` adds `faf_editor`, which links the engine's object library `faf_engine` whole, as the engine tests do, so the engine's objects are linked in the game's order. The game's `WinMain` is one of them, so the editor starts at its own `main()` (`/ENTRY:mainCRTStartup`). It carries the game's window resources and manifest and the DXC DLLs as the game does.
- **The game inside:** the editor's app (`EdApp.cpp`) holds the game's own `CScApp` and runs the game's loop (`WIN_AppExecute`), with what the game's `WinMain` sets up around it (the crash report's die handler, the accessibility hotkeys turned off and restored). The window, device, user interface, sound and world sessions are the game's, unchanged. Engine additions in `CScApp`, unused by the game: `moho::gAppRenderers` (Direct3D 12 and Vulkan only, no Direct3D 9/10 fallback), `moho::gAppWindowed` (one normal window; `SC_PrimaryAdapter` leaves it alone) and `moho::gAppBeforeDeviceCreate` (the editor's second head). The game's first-frame start (main menu or command-line session) is skipped; sessions start from Play.
- **Two heads:** the device has the game's head 0 on the game's viewport, which the editor keeps over the middle of its dock layout, and a head 1 on the editor's own child window under it, for the panels. The game draws only head 0 (its viewport was made for one head); the editor draws head 1 each frame, and the game's paint presents both. When the layout or the window changes, the viewport is moved and both heads are resized (`CD3DDevice::InitContext`). The game's interface lays itself out in the viewport, as in the game.
- **One picture:** the game's view is shown inside the panels' picture, not through its own window: before the panels draw, head 0's back buffer (the frame the game drew last, not yet presented: `CD3DDevice::Paint` presents, then draws the next) is copied with `StretchRect` into a target of the view's size, and the panels draw it under all their windows. The viewport's window keeps its place with an empty region, and the panels' window hands it the mouse (in its own coordinates), the cursor and, on a click, the keyboard wherever no popup or floating window covers it. So popups, tooltips and floating windows over the view move with it in one picture; through the window's own picture they lagged a frame or two behind. `/nocomposite` shows the view's window again, with holes cut where popups reach over it.
- **Panels:** [Dear ImGui](https://github.com/ocornut/imgui) 1.92.4, docking branch, pinned in `cmake/FafDependencies.cmake` and built by `cmake/deps/imgui`, drawn by `ui/EdImGuiRenderer.cpp` on gal's GPU programs (`gpg/gal/GpuDriven.h`): one HLSL source DXC builds for both APIs. Its input is the panels' window's; F5 is the editor's in either window.
- **Play** (`EdPlay.cpp`): the editor's Lua launcher builds the session info as FAF's quick start does and calls `LaunchSinglePlayerSession`; `WLD_Teardown` ends the session, before the game's frame would go on to its main menu. A Lua request to quit during play (`ExitApplication`) ends the session, not the editor.
- **Older renderers:** their DLLs are delay-loaded (`d3d9.dll`, `d3dx9_43.dll`, `d3d10.dll`, `d3d11.dll`), so the editor starts without the June 2010 DirectX runtime and never loads Direct3D 9. Direct3D 11 still loads when Direct3D 12 or Vulkan decode a texture: they do it on a private WARP device ([Renderer backends](RENDERER_BACKENDS.md)).

## Known issues

- Each Play still keeps a little private memory after it ends: over 20 plays of SCMP_009 on Direct3D 12 the lowest point after a play rose 1248 to 1380 MB, 82 MB of it the first play's warm-up, then about 1.9 MB a play, 0.7 MB over the last ten (it was 15 MB a play). The heap, the Lua range and the GPU's memory stay flat once warm; what grows is in the graphics driver's write-combined blocks. Freed before: a sync packet's shared references (the sim's height field, 4 MB), the world meshes the game UI never destroys, the cursor each UI state switch made, the Lua range's unmerged free blocks, the particle buffers a play first used (FAF's 4096-buffer pool was handed out oldest first), and the UI Lua state's garbage, now collected after each play.
- The editor started by a tool that can't reach the desktop's browser (Windows reports no association) can't open FAF's sign-in page; the card shows the link to copy instead.
